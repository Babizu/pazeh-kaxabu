## What is included

This dictionary brings together sources on Pazeh and Kaxabu: the *Pazih Dictionary* by Paul Jen-kuei Li and Shigeru Tsuchida,
the Kaxabu classified dictionary, example sentences and recordings from Lin Hong-rui's *Kaxabu Reference Grammar*,
Pan Dexing's word list and video corpus, and early word lists from 19th-century Western publications.
Every source has its own format; each one is converted into the same standard format by its own adapter,
keeping the original spelling and a precise citation.

The scope, processing and known limitations of each source are described under [Sources](#/sources).

## Dialect labels

<p><span class="variety-badge variety-pazeh">Pazeh</span> <span class="variety-badge variety-kaxabu">Kaxabu</span> <span class="variety-badge variety-auran">Pazeh (Auran)</span></p>

The *Pazih Dictionary* marks dialects with [A], [K] and [P]. Its [K] and [P] labels are the reverse of the usual names,
so they have been swapped here: **[A] → Pazeh (Auran), [K] → Pazeh, [P] → Kaxabu**.
The original letter is kept in each entry's “original label” field for checking.

Auran is a local variety of Pazeh rather than a separate language, so it is shown as “Pazeh (Auran)” in a lighter shade
of the same colour. **Filtering by Pazeh also includes entries marked Auran**; selecting only Pazeh (Auran) shows Auran entries alone.

Other sources are labelled according to what the source itself says; when the dialect is uncertain, no label is given.

## Spelling and writing systems

The sources use different spelling systems: the *Pazih Dictionary*, the Kaxabu teaching orthography, the Pan Yong-li orthography
(with tone marks, e.g. <span class="native-text">pù nu</span>) and the transcriptions of early publications
(e.g. <span class="native-text">tūhūbū́ss</span>). Words are always displayed as in the source; when searching, the dictionary automatically:

- ignores case and diacritics (except Kaxabu é)
- treats apostrophes (’ ʼ → ') and ǝ/ə alike
- ignores dictionary notation: hyphens, equals signs and infix brackets (`sikis-`, `ha=ka`, `<in>`)

## Cross-dialect fuzzy search

Search uses a multi-character weighted edit distance: an ordinary substitution, deletion or insertion costs 1.5, 1.0 or 0.8,
while systematic sound correspondences between the dialects cost only 0.1. For example:

- <span class="native-text">semer ≈ semee</span>　flap er↔ee
- <span class="native-text">rapay ≈ apay</span>　dropped r
- <span class="native-text">raulu ≈ laulu</span>　r↔l
- <span class="native-text">bintul ≈ bintun</span>　final l↔n

To see the calculation step by step, open the [Algorithm Lab](#/lab).

## Citing

This dictionary is only an interface for combining and searching the data; the copyright and scholarship belong to the original sources.
Please cite the original source: the “Source” card on every entry gives the page, number or timecode and has a “Copy citation” button,
and entries from the *Pazih Dictionary* can be opened next to the scanned page.
